5 · Nowcasting · the 0–6 hour problem

The next six hours: small, short-lived storms on the move

  • A thunderstorm cell is a few to tens of km across and lives tens of minutes to a few hours. A 25 km global grid cannot see it; you need a km-scale, convection-allowing model.
  • Nowcasting = forecasting the next 0–6 h. The classic baseline is extrapolation: estimate how radar echoes move and slide the latest image forward. Good for the first hour; it cannot grow, weaken or start a storm.
  • Km-scale NWP (e.g. NOAA's 3 km HRRR) simulates storms, but its first hours suffer from spin-up: it must build clouds and updrafts its initial state describes poorly.
  • Earth-2 learns the storm-scale evolution instead: StormCast from an NWP model's output, StormScope straight from satellite and radar.

StormScope = Earth-2 Nowcasting

StormScope learns storms from satellite and radar images

  • GOES satellite bands and the MRMS radar mosaic over CONUS at 6 km, no NWP initial state. A transformer diffusion model generates the next state; each run is one sample.
  • Nowcasting: 0–2 h in 10-min steps from the past hour of observations. Nearcasting: 0–12 h in 1-h steps, steered by one GFS channel.

Knowing that storms die and form beats just moving them

Persistence: the issue-time map, unchanged. Advection: motion from cross-correlating two radar frames 30 min apart, then the map slides forward. Generative ensemble (16 members, a toy stand-in for a learned model): each member moves the cells, guesses each cell's remaining life with some noise, and draws new cells at random. CSI = hits / (hits + misses + false alarms) above a threshold.
