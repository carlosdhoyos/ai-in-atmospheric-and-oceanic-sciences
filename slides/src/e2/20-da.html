3 · Data assimilation · why initial conditions matter

A forecast is only as good as its starting state, and errors in it grow

  • The atmosphere is chaotic: two states that differ by a tiny amount drift apart, slowly at first and then exponentially, until they are as different as two random states.
  • The toy is the Lorenz-63 system, three equations for convection. The same model runs twice; the second start differs from the first by the slider's amount in x.
  • The analysis is the best estimate of the current state, built from observations and a short forecast. Producing it is called data assimilation (DA). Every forecast, physics or AI, starts from one.

Classical analysis · optimal interpolation

The analysis weighs background and observations by their error variances

xa = xb + K (y − H xb),   K = B Hᵀ (H B Hᵀ + R)⁻¹

The background xb is a short forecast from the last analysis, y the observations, H maps the grid to the observation points (here: linear interpolation). B, R: background and observation error covariances. One observation on a grid point: K = σb² / (σb² + σo²), so trust whichever is less wrong. The correlation length L in B spreads each correction to the neighbours.

Observations · what the system actually sees

Observations are scattered in space and time; each instrument sees a different piece

  • Polar orbiters fly pole to pole and scan a strip below them; Earth turns underneath, so the gaps fill only over hours. They carry the microwave and infrared sounders that matter most for DA.
  • Geostationary satellites stay over one point on the equator and image the same disk again and again, but never see the poles.
  • Radiosondes: balloons launched at 00 and 12 UTC, mostly over Northern Hemisphere land. Aircraft, ships, buoys and GNSS radio occultation add more.
  • Satellites measure radiances, so classical DA needs an observation operator H (a radiative-transfer model) per instrument.

The HEALPix grid · equal-area pixels on the sphere

HEALPix: pixels of equal area, sitting on rings of latitude

  • HEALPix (Hierarchical Equal Area isoLatitude Pixelization): 12 base pixels, each split into Nside × Nside: 12 Nside² pixels. Doubling Nside splits every pixel into 4 (hierarchical).
  • Every pixel has the same area, so none is over-counted in a loss or a global mean. Lat-lon cells shrink to slivers at the poles.
  • Pixel centres lie on rings of constant latitude (fast spherical-harmonic transforms), and the 12 faces tile into square patches that ordinary convolutions can use.
  • Used by HealDA (v1 analysis on a 1° HEALPix grid), DLESyM (12 faces × 64 × 64 = 49,152 cells, ~110 km) and cBottle (HPX64 ~100 km → HPX1024 ~5 km).

Four questions on initial conditions, analyses and grids

Key ideas

  • Chaos makes initial errors grow exponentially: better analyses buy forecast time.
  • A classical analysis blends a background forecast and observations, weighted by their error variances and correlations (B, R, H).
  • Observations are irregular and indirect; coverage depends on the time window.
  • HEALPix: equal-area pixels on latitude rings (HealDA, DLESyM, cBottle).
  • HealDA learns observations → analysis without a background; reported cost < 1 day of lead time vs ERA5 starts.
