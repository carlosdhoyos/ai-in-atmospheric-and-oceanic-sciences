Paper §4.2.2: 195 M parameters in total, ~70 GB GPU memory in training, ~5 GB and 33 s per forecast step (100 sampling steps, fp32, one H100) at inference; d = 768, 16 blocks, 28,672 tokens, NA 31 × 31. Not stated: heads, MLP width, positional embedding, output head, training precision. Our estimate uses the DiT recipe (Peebles & Xie 2023): heads of 64, MLP 4 d, adaLN-Zero (6 d per block from a d-vector), 256-frequency σ embedding + 2-layer MLP, final adaLN + linear; activations: ~(12 + 2·r) d floats per token per block + attention weights. The "learned table" option is our way to reconcile the count, not a paper fact.

What the paper says

        
paperOne fixed domain over the continental US on the HRRR Lambert grid: 512 × 896 cells of 6 km ≈ 3,072 × 5,376 km (§4.1, §4.2.2). The paper itself calls it "the limited-area domain" (§4.2.1). Scores are computed inside the CONUS land boundary (§2.2.1).

        
paperFor 0–12 h nearcasts, large-scale context comes from one GFS field (Z500 or simulated reflectivity), "given the limited-area domain and the relatively long forecast lead time" (§4.2.1). Nowcasts (0–2 h) have none.

        
our reasoningNothing outside the box enters the network. A storm upstream of the boundary is invisible at t = 0, so near the inflow edge the forecast must invent what comes in. The band of affected cells grows as wind × lead time (green: cells whose upstream source was inside the box).

      

      
    

  

  
Toy: storms as dots moved by one uniform steering wind; no growth, decay or new storms. It shows only the geometry of an open boundary, not a model result. A real model also sees clues near the edge (anvils, moisture, the GFS field in nearcast mode), so the "trustworthy interior" is our simplification, not something the paper measures.
