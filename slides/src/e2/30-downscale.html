Downscaling turns a coarse field (a global model or reanalysis such as ERA5, ~25 km) into a km-scale map. Cold valleys, ridges, sea breezes and showers live below the coarse grid spacing. Interpolation only fills in between coarse values: it adds no variance at scales the input never resolved.

Toy stand-ins, not the real networks: μ = the truth blurred to the coarse scale; r = a fresh random field with the same power spectrum as the true residual (truth − μ), one per noise draw.

A diffusion toy · denoising, score, noise schedule

Start from pure noise; remove a little at each step; large scales appear first

Training: add noise of level σ to real residuals; teach a network D(x; σ) to recover the clean field (a denoiser; D(x) − x points toward the data: the score). Sampling: start from pure noise (σ large) and walk σ down to 0, each step moving x toward the guess.

xi+1 = xi + (σi+1 − σi) · (xi − D(xi; σi)) / σi

Power spectra · where the variance sits, scale by scale

Regression alone wins on RMSE but loses the small scales; diffusion puts their power back

The power spectrum splits a field's variance by wavelength. Real weather keeps power down to the grid scale. A field that is too smooth falls off a cliff to the right of the coarse-grid limit.

Speed claims · read the baseline first

“22×”, “500×”, “1,000×” faster: the sources disagree, so read each with its baseline

Each number below is a claim about CorrDiff against a physics model, as stated by its source. They span more than an order of magnitude. A speed-up is a ratio, so it depends on both sides: which NWP run, on how many CPU cores, at what resolution, against which GPU, counting what.

click each bar to read its baseline; switch to a linear scale and see the paper's own figure almost vanish.

Before you quote a speed-up, ask: what was the baseline model and hardware? Same resolution and domain? One sample or an ensemble? Is training cost included? A fair line for a report: “NVIDIA reports up to 500× faster; the peer-reviewed paper reports at least 22× against WRF on 928 CPU cores.”

Sources: Mardani et al., arXiv:2309.15214 v4 (at least 22× faster, 1,300× more energy-efficient than WRF on 928 CPU cores); NVIDIA blog, Jun 2024 (1,000× faster, 3,000× more energy-efficient); SC24 NIM announcement and Earth-2 page (500× faster, 10,000× energy efficiency); NVIDIA blog 26 Jan 2026 (up to 500× faster; Israel Meteorological Service: 90% less compute time at 2.5 km vs classic NWP on a CPU cluster).

Five questions on CorrDiff

Take-aways
  • Interpolation cannot create variance below the coarse grid spacing.
  • A regression network trained with MSE gives the conditional mean: lowest RMSE, too smooth.
  • CorrDiff adds a diffusion-generated residual: realistic spectra, and samples that agree at large scale and differ in detail.
  • Diffusion builds a field from noise in steps, coarse scales first.
  • Speed-ups are claims against a baseline; the paper and the vendor posts differ by more than 20×.
