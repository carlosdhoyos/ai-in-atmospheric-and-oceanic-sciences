South–north cut through the storms (toy, dBZ)

A model trained to minimise the mean squared error (MSE) predicts the conditional mean of the possible futures: it is the best single guess on average, but no single future looks like it. A generative model learns the whole distribution p(future | past) and draws one plausible future per sample; StormScope does this with a diffusion model (paper §4.2.3: it "learns the conditional distribution p(xt+ | xt−; c)"). Toy: one storm on a 40 × 70 grid of 6 km cells, peak 50 dBZ; averages taken in dBZ.

5b · StormScope · diffusion from scratch · 2 · the forward process: adding noise

Adding noise xσ = x + σ·n destroys the small scales first; the large ones survive longest

Power spectrum: how much variance at each scale

Histogram of pixel values

step σ through 0.002 → 80 with the chips: watch where the noisy spectrum (blue) leaves the clean one (black). Everything to the right of that point is drowned.

Noise is white: the same variance σ² at every scale. Weather fields have most variance at large scales, so the small scales sink below the noise first. Denoising therefore rebuilds the large scales at high σ and the details at low σ.

Forward process as in EDM (Karras et al. 2022): xσ = x + σ·n with n ~ N(0, I), the same as eq. (1) of the paper (nσ ~ N(0, σ²I), §4.2.3). σ range 0.002–80 and σdata = 0.5 are EDM defaults (the paper does not state its values). Toy: a 128 × 128 storm field (6 km cells, 768 km across), scaled to standard deviation 0.5; "rescaled" divides by √(σ² + σdata²), which is what EDM's input scaling cin does before the network sees the image.

5b · StormScope · diffusion from scratch · 3 · the denoiser

The best possible denoiser returns a weighted average of all clean images that could explain the noisy one

D(xσ; σ) = Σi wi xi,   wi ∝ exp(−‖xσ − xi‖² / 2σ²)
  • The denoiser D gets a noisy image and σ, and must guess the clean image. Trained with squared error, its best answer is the average of the candidates, weighted by how well each one explains the noisy image.
  • High σ: every candidate fits equally badly → equal weights → the dataset mean (blurry).
  • Low σ: one candidate fits far better → weight ≈ 1 → a sharp image.
Noise image #

slide σ slowly from 20 down to 0.01: at what σ does the denoiser "decide" which of the 8 storms it is looking at?

Toy: 8 "training" futures of the same scene (32 × 56 cells, dBZ/60), exact optimal denoiser for this finite set (the posterior mean under equal prior weights). With 1,792 pixels pooling evidence, the noisy image still identifies its source at σ ≈ 1. A real network never sees the whole training set at once: it learns a smooth approximation of this average and can also produce futures that are not in the training set. StormScope trains this denoiser with eq. (1) of the paper (§4.2.3).

5b · StormScope · diffusion from scratch · 4 · a picture with two pixels

With two pixels, every image is a point; the denoiser becomes a field of arrows towards the data

  • Image = (rain at pixel A, rain at pixel B). Training data: 3 kinds of future, storm over A, storm over B, no storm (9 examples each).
  • Shading: the density of noisy images pσ. Arrows: D(x; σ) − x, the step to the denoiser's guess. Divided by σ², it is the score ∇ log pσ: the uphill direction of that density.
  • Diffusion sampling = start at high σ, then follow the arrows while lowering σ.

set σ to 1 and click anywhere on the map: the point follows the arrows as σ shrinks and lands on one kind of future. Hover to see the denoiser's guess and its weights.

Toy: 27 data points in 2-D, exact denoiser of this set; σ range 0.01–3 (toy). Points move with the probability-flow ODE of EDM (Karras et al. 2022, eq. 1 with σ(t) = t): dx/dσ = (x − D(x; σ)) / σ. In StormScope a point x is a whole future frame: 512 × 896 = 458,752 numbers per channel (§4.2.2), so 8 GOES channels (Table 1) make a point in a space of ~3.7 million dimensions; the arrows are the same idea.

Everything runs in this page: a 2-hidden-layer network with SiLU activations, written by hand (forward pass, backpropagation, Adam), inputs (cin·x, cnoise), EDM pre-conditioning D = cskip·x + cout·F and EDM loss weight λ(σ) (Karras et al. 2022; σdata = 0.5). σ is drawn uniform in log σ as in the paper (§4.2.3), here between 0.01 and 4 (toy). Sampling: start from pure noise at σmax and step σ down to 0, following the denoiser

80 samples in the two-pixel world

Pixel A of each sample, step by step

Sampler

set N = 1, then 2, 4, 12: with too few steps the samples land between the clusters (grey).

EDM samplers (Karras et al. 2022, Alg. 1–2): Euler takes the slope (x − D)/σ at the start of a step; Heun averages it with the slope at the end (2 network calls per step, 1 on the last); "churn" adds a little fresh noise before each step. Schedule σi = (σmax1/ρ + i/(N−1)·(σmin1/ρ − σmax1/ρ))ρ, ρ = 7 (EDM default), then σ = 0. Toy: σmax = 4, σmin = 0.01, exact denoiser of a 3-cluster distribution (no storm 50 %, A 25 %, B 25 %). StormScope: "ODE-based deterministic sampler with stochastic perturbations", 100 steps from σmax to σmin (paper §4.2.3).

More:

Paper §4.2.3: EDM framework (Karras et al. 2022), loss eq. (1) with "the standard EDM loss weighting" λ(σ) and EDM pre-conditioning "to ensure unit variance across the range of noise levels"; σ "uniform in log(σ)" within [σmin, σmax]; coarse and fine experts split at σint (refs. 46–47: eDiff-I, cBottle); sampler: EDM ODE with stochastic perturbations, 100 steps, expert switch at σint. Not stated in the paper: σmin, σmax, σint, σdata, ρ, churn settings. Shown: EDM defaults σmin = 0.002, σmax = 80, σdata = 0.5, ρ = 7, log-normal Pmean = −1.2, Pstd = 1.2 (Karras et al. 2022, Table 1); σint = 1 is a toy value.
