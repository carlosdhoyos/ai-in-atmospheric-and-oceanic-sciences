2 · Global forecasting · autoregressive rollout

A forecast is one 6-hour step fed back into itself: small errors grow

An AI global model learns a single map: the state at t → the state at t + 6 h. To reach day 10 it applies that map 40 times to its own output (autoregressive rollout). Here the "truth" is a toy 500 hPa field of drifting Rossby-like waves; the toy model gets each wave's speed slightly wrong, more so for the small waves, so the error compounds with every step.

play the rollout, then set the step error to 0.5 and to 3: on which day does the ACC fall below 0.6, and when does the forecast's RMSE pass the climatology line?

Real models: FourCastNet steps 6 h at 0.25° (arXiv:2202.11214); SFNO stays stable for 1,460 six-hour steps = 1 year (arXiv:2306.03838).

FourCastNet (2022) · Adaptive Fourier Neural Operator

Mixing in Fourier space: every longitude talks to every other in one step

A vision transformer mixes its patches ("tokens") with attention, whose cost grows with the square of the number of tokens. AFNO instead takes an FFT across the tokens, multiplies each wavenumber by learned weights (a small MLP per mode), drops the high modes, and transforms back. Here: one latitude circle of the toy field, 128 longitudes.

x → FFT → Xk → Wk·Xk (keep k ≤ K) → iFFT → x′

with W = 1, lower K from 40 to 3 and watch the curve lose its small waves; then switch to the learned W: one complex number per mode moves every wave one day east.

Toy: Wk is fitted by least squares on pairs (day d, day d + 1) for 20 days at this latitude. FourCastNet: AFNO token mixing (FFT → per-mode MLP → iFFT) on a ViT patch backbone, 0.25° global grid (721 × 1440), 6-h step, 20 variables, ERA5 1979–2015; a week-long forecast in < 2 s; the authors estimate ~45,000× faster per node-hour than IFS (vs an extrapolated 18 km IFS) (Pathak et al., arXiv:2202.11214).

SFNO (2023) · spherical Fourier neural operator

The Earth is not a flat periodic rectangle: SFNO mixes in spherical harmonics

A 2-D FFT on the lat-lon grid treats the map as a flat sheet that wraps around top to bottom. But the top row is a single point (the pole), a grid cell at 80° is 6 times narrower than at the equator, and the north edge does not touch the south edge. SFNO replaces the FFT by the spherical harmonic transform: basis patterns Yℓm that live on the sphere, with no edges and no special poles.

compare a harmonic with a flat-FFT mode near the pole (drag the globe to look down on it); then move the round storm from 0° to 85° and read how many grid columns it covers.

Spherical harmonics are drawn as real parts Pℓm(sin φ) cos(mλ); a flat-FFT mode is cos(mλ)·cos(2πn·row/rows), periodic top to bottom. SFNO / FourCastNet v2: the lat-lon FFT "incorrectly assumes a flat geometry" (artefacts, dissipation); 73 channels, ERA5 0.25°, 6 h; stable rollouts for 1 year (1,460 steps), a year-long rollout in 12.8 min on one A6000; skill comparable to IFS to ~2 weeks (Bonev et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2306.03838).

Deterministic models at long lead · the mean of possible futures

Trained on mean-squared error, a model learns to blur

At long lead the future is uncertain: many states are possible. A network trained to minimise the mean-squared error (MSE) is rewarded for predicting their average, and the average of many sharp, shifted waves is a smooth, weak wave. The small scales go first, because their position is lost first. Each member below is as sharp as the truth.

Toy: each member shifts every wave by a random phase whose spread grows with lead time and wavenumber. The mean of the members is what an MSE-trained deterministic model converges to. This is why FourCastNet 3 is trained as an ensemble and reports "realistic spectra to 60 days" (arXiv:2507.12144).

FourCastNet 3 (2025) · ensembles scored by CRPS

CRPS rewards an ensemble that is both sharp and honest about its spread

CRPS = E|X − y| − ½ E|X − X′| = ∫ (F(x) − 1[x ≥ y])² dx

The continuous ranked probability score compares the ensemble's CDF F with the observation y. The first term pulls members towards y; the second rewards spread. Blurring every member does not help: CRPS is minimised, on average, only when members are drawn from the true distribution.

drag the observation: an under-dispersed ensemble wins when y is near its centre and loses badly in the tails. On the right, find the spread that gives the lowest average CRPS.

Toy: 16 members placed at Gaussian quantiles (16 is FCN3's training ensemble size). FourCastNet 3: probabilistic, convolutional on the sphere (spherical-harmonic filters + local DISCO convolutions), ~711M parameters, 72 variables, 0.25°, 6 h; trained with point-wise CRPS plus a spectral CRPS (CRPS on the spherical-harmonic coefficients, so members keep the right power at each scale); 50 members in evaluation; 15-day forecast ≈ 60 s on one H100; ~8× faster than GenCast, ~60× than IFS-ENS; beats IFS-ENS, close to GenCast (Bonev, Kurth et al., arXiv:2507.12144).
