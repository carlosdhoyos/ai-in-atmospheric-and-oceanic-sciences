5b · The grid

Every source goes onto one 6 km grid, one frame every 10 minutes

click the four stages in order: where do the 512 × 896 pixels come from?

ABI remapped to the HRRR 3 km Lambert conformal conic grid, then downsampled to 6 km; MRMS interpolated to the same grid at a 10-min cadence (§4.1.1); GFS/ERA5 at 0.25° bilinearly interpolated to 3 km then 6 km (§4.1.2); model grid 512 × 896 (§4.2.2); 3 km domain 1792 × 1024 ≈ 1.8 M pixels (§6.2); parallax (§3); GOES and radar every 2–4 min over CONUS (§4.2.1).

5b · The grid · why 6 km, and what it costs

What grid spacing does to a storm: small cells vanish, peaks drop

Averaging a storm onto coarser pixels (in linear units of Z, as radar power adds) smooths its cores. StormScope runs at 6 km: fine enough to keep most convective structure, coarse enough for 512 × 896 pixels per channel on one GPU.

move from 3 to 24 km: when do the small cells vanish, what happens to the peak, and how many storm objects survive the 108 km² rule?

6 km grid 512 × 896 (§4.2.2); the 3 km model (1792 × 1024) needed domain parallelism: 2 GPUs per model, ~87.5 % scaling efficiency (§6.2); pySTEPS can run at the radar's native 1–2 km while StormScope runs at 6 km (§3); storm objects ≥ 40 dBZ with area > 108 km² (§2.2.2, following WoFSCast). The storm is a toy built at 1.5 km over 504 × 288 km.
