5b · StormScope step by step

StormScope in one picture: recent satellite and radar images in, an ensemble of future images out

StormScope (Earth-2 Nowcasting) forecasts the observations themselves: the next GOES satellite image and the next radar image, 10 minutes or 1 hour ahead, again and again. There is no weather-model state and no data assimilation in the loop.

5b · The grid · from a satellite view to 512 × 896 pixels

Every source is put on one 6 km grid over the US, one frame every 10 minutes

move the grid spacing from 3 to 24 km: when do the small cells vanish, and what happens to the peak reflectivity?

ABI remapped to the HRRR 3 km Lambert conformal grid, then downsampled to 6 km; MRMS interpolated to the same grid; 10-min cadence (§4.1.1); GFS/ERA5 Z500 at 0.25° bilinearly interpolated to 3 km and then 6 km (§4.1.2); model grid 512 × 896 (§4.2.2); 3 km version 1792 × 1024 (§6.2). Storm objects: ≥ 40 dBZ and area > 108 km² (§2.2.2). The storm is a toy built at 1.5 km and averaged in linear Z units.

5b · Two modes · nowcasting and nearcasting

Nowcast: six recent frames, 10-minute steps to 2 h. Nearcast: one frame plus one GFS field, 1-hour steps to 12 h

in the nowcast, move to step 6 and then 7: when does the input window stop containing any real observation? Then switch to the nearcast.

Table 2, §4.2.1, §4.2.3 and Fig. 11 of arXiv:2601.17268: nowcast input = 6 states 10 min apart over the preceding hour; nearcast input = one state 1 h earlier; GFS Z500 (ERA5 in training) conditions the satellite nearcast, GFS simulated composite reflectivity the radar nearcast. 33 s per step on one H100 → ~7 min for a 2-h nowcast or 12-h nearcast (§4.2.2).

5b · Two stages and a sliding window

Each step runs the satellite model first, then the radar model on its output; the forecasts slide into the input window

Fig. 11 and §4.2.1 of arXiv:2601.17268: predicted x̃(t), ỹ(t) are recycled as inputs (autoregressive sliding window); the satellite models are trained and sampled independently of the radar models, which are conditioned on satellite inputs in training and on the satellite forecast at inference (§4.1.2), so the approach also works without radar; multimodal forecasts run the two models in sequence each step (§4.2.2).
