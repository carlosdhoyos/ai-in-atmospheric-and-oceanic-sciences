5b · StormScope · inside the network · 2 · neighborhood attention

Each token looks only at its 31 × 31 neighbours; depth carries information further

Window

click a token in the middle, then one in a corner, and raise the layers: how many layers until one token can "see" the whole domain?

  • Self-attention: every token makes a query, compares it with the keys of other tokens, and takes a weighted mix of their values.
  • Global attention compares all pairs: N² = 28,672². Neighborhood attention (NA) compares each token only with a window around it: N · 31².
  • At the edges the window shifts inwards, so every token still sees 31 × 31 tokens.

Attention scores per layer and head

Paper §4.2.2: 2-D Neighborhood Attention with a 31 × 31 window "greatly reducing the compute required for self-attention while allowing long-range dependencies to be captured gradually over multiple layers"; 16 blocks; 128 × 224 tokens; one token = 4 × 4 pixels of 6 km = 24 km. NA and its edge behaviour: Hassani et al. (CVPR 2023, NeurIPS 2024) [paper refs 42–44]. Receptive field = our arithmetic: (31 − 1) × layers + 1 tokens, clipped to the domain. Background: toy IR image.

5b · StormScope · verification · 7 · fractions skill score

FSS: compare rain fractions in windows, so a near miss is not punished twice

Pixels above threshold

Threshold
FSS = 1 − Σ (Pf − Po)² / [ Σ Pf² + Σ Po² ]

Pf, Po: fraction of pixels above the threshold in an n × n window around each pixel, in the forecast and the observation. FSS = 1 is perfect; 0 means no overlap at that scale.

shift the forecast by 18 km with a 6 km window: pixel scores collapse. Now widen the window: from what size is the forecast "useful"?

FSS: Roberts & Lean 2008 (paper ref 32); "useful" when FSS ≥ 0.5 + f0/2, f0 = observed base rate (their criterion). The paper uses FSS with windows of 18 km at 20 dBZ and 42 km at 30 dBZ for nearcasts (§2.2.1, Fig. 6), 6/18/30 km at 40 dBZ for nowcasts (§2.2.2, Fig. 9), and 6 km at 235 K / 225 K for IR (Fig. 7). Toy: the forecast is the observation shifted east; 6 km pixels.

5b · StormScope · 10 · cost and scaling

33 s per step on one GPU; at 3 km the domain is split across GPUs

Mode
Models

set 24 members for a 2-h multimodal nowcast: how many GPUs keep it inside the 10-minute cycle of new images?

Where the 33 s go

One forecast step = one full diffusion sampling run: 100 denoising steps of the 195 M-parameter DiT over 28,672 tokens, ≈ 0.33 s per step (fp32, H100). The authors expect large speed-ups from distillation (fewer sampling steps), domain parallelism and reduced precision (§4.2.2).

The 3 km model: domain parallelism, halo exchange

step through ①–⑤: which operation is the only one that needs data from the other GPU?

Paper §4.2.2: ~5 GB GPU memory and 33 s per forecast step at 100 sampling steps (fp32, one H100) → ~7 min for a 12-h nearcast or a 2-h nowcast; multimodal runs take longer (models in sequence; we assume 2×); training ~70 GB, 32 H100 × ~48 h. §6.2, Figs. 14–15: 3 km satellite nowcast on 1792 × 1024 (≈ 1.8 M pixels) exceeds ~80 GB even at batch 1; ShardTensor (PhysicsNeMo) splits the domain into upper and lower halves; halo width set by the NA kernel; domain group 2 × data-parallel 4; ~87.5 % scaling efficiency. Our assumptions: time ∝ sampling steps; members spread evenly over GPUs.
