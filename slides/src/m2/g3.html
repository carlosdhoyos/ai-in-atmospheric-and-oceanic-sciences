09Recurrence (simple RNN)

A recurrent cell reads one day at a time and carries a memory forward

ht = tanh(a · ht−1 + b · xt)

Worked example: a = 0.5, b = 1, h₀ = 0, rainfall [1, 0, 2]. Day 1: tanh(1) = 0.76. Day 2: tanh(0.38) = 0.36. Day 3: tanh(0.18 + 2) = 0.97.

  • a is the memory weight: high a keeps the past, low a forgets fast.
  • b is the input weight: how strongly today's rain pushes the state.
  • The same a and b are reused at every step.
Vanishing memory. With a = 0.5, a past day's influence roughly halves each step: 0.5¹⁰ ≈ 0.001 after 10 days. LSTMs were built to fix this. Saturation: tanh flattens out, so inputs above about 3 all give nearly 1.

10Gated recurrence (LSTM, GRU)

Gates are valves that decide what the memory keeps, adds and releases

ct = f · ct−1 + i · gt    ht = o · tanh(ct)
  • Forget gate f (0 to 1): how much of last month's cell state to keep. f = 0.9 keeps 90%.
  • Input gate i: how much of the new candidate g (from this month's rain) to add. A wet month opens it and raises the stored state.
  • Output gate o: how much of the memory to release as the output h, here the streamflow signal.

Over the long dry spell, the cell state drains slowly, like a basin's groundwater. A simple RNN with a = 0.5 has forgotten the wet season within a few months.

An LSTM is a composite of simpler operations: weighted sums, sigmoid gates, tanh, element-wise multiply and add. It is used as one layer, and Module 3 treats it there. A GRU does the same with two gates.

11Self-attention

Each word scores every word, softmax turns scores into weights, and the output is a weighted average

  • Query: what am I looking for? Key: what do I contain? Value: what do I pass on? Each comes from its own learned dense layer.
  • Score = q · k / √d, with d the vector length, so softmax does not become too sharp.
w = softmax(q·k / √d)    out = Σ wj vj

Example: "it" against [the, cat, sat, tired] has scores [0, 3, 1, 2]. Softmax gives ≈ [0.03, 0.64, 0.09, 0.24], so "it" takes its meaning mostly from "cat".

Cost grows with the square of the length. Every element scores every other one.
This is what motivates state space models (operation 26).

11bMulti-head attention

Several heads attend in parallel, each to a different kind of relation

Each head has its own query, key and value weights. The head outputs are concatenated and mixed by a dense layer.

A vector of 512 numbers split into 8 heads of 64. Each head works on its 64-number slice.

  • Head 1: the last few days (persistence).
  • Head 2: the same date last year (seasonality).
  • Head 3: days with a similar pressure pattern (analogues).
One head makes one weighted average and can focus on one kind of relation. Several heads look at several relations at once for about the same cost.

11cCausal masking

A causal mask stops each day from attending to the future

Before the softmax, scores for future positions are set to minus infinity, so their weights become exactly 0. Each day attends only to itself and the past.

scoret,s = −∞  for s > t

Example: day 3 has scores [1, 2, 0.5, 3, 4] for days 1 to 5. Mask days 4 and 5, then softmax over [1, 2, 0.5] ≈ [0.23, 0.63, 0.14], and 0 for days 4 and 5.

Data leakage, built into the architecture. Module 1 warned against letting future information into the inputs. The mask enforces that rule inside the model. It is also what lets language models and forecasting transformers generate one step at a time.

12Cross-attention

A station's forecast attends to the satellite cells upwind of it

In cross-attention the queries come from one sequence and the keys and values from another. Here the query is a station, and the keys and values are the cells of a satellite cloud field over Colombia.

w = softmax(qstation · kcell)   out = Σ w · vcell

A trained model learns that clouds arriving at the station come from upwind. Here the score is set by hand: it falls with distance and rises for cells in the direction the wind comes from.
