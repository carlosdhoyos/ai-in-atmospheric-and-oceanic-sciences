13Graph message passing

Each station updates itself from the messages of its neighbours

hi ← α · hi + (1 − α) · meanj ∈ N(i) hj

Stations are nodes, edges say who talks to whom. Station A has 10 mm and neighbours with 20 and 30 mm: new A = 0.5 · 10 + 0.5 · mean(20, 30) = 17.5 mm. Real graph networks put learned weights on the messages; this keeps only the averaging.

  • After k rounds a station holds information from stations k hops away (ringed on the map).
  • Too many rounds and every station looks alike: over-smoothing. Watch the spread fall.

14Pooling

Pooling summarizes each neighbourhood with one number and has no weights

Slide a window over the grid and keep its maximum (max pooling) or its mean (average pooling). With a 2 × 2 window and stride 2 a 4 × 4 grid becomes 2 × 2.

  • No weights: nothing to learn, only a fixed rule.
  • Shift tolerance: if the peak moves inside its window, the max does not change.
  • Global average pooling averages the whole map. Many CNNs use it to turn a stack of maps into one feature vector.

15Upsampling and downsampling

Shrink a field to see context, grow it back to draw a detailed map

  • Downsampling: pooling or a strided convolution keeps one value per f × f block.
  • Upsampling: copy the nearest value, interpolate (bilinear), or learn the weights with a transposed convolution.
  • U-Net shrinks a precipitation field to capture context, then grows it back to a pixel-level map. Skip connections bring the fine detail across.

16Flatten and reshape

Flatten and reshape move numbers around without changing any of them

A 2 × 2 grid becomes a list of 4, and reshaping brings it back:

[[1, 2],[3, 4]]→ 1234→ [[1, 2], [3, 4]]
  • No arithmetic, no weights. Only the shape label changes.
  • Flatten turns a map from a convolution into a vector a dense layer can read.
  • The order is a convention. Row-major (NumPy, PyTorch) reads row by row. Mix conventions and the map is scrambled.
A 4 × 4 grid holds 16 numbers whatever its shape: 4 × 4, 1 × 16 or 2 × 8.

17Embedding lookup

A category ID picks one row from a table of learnable vectors

125 municipalities of Antioquia, each with 4 numbers: a 125 × 4 table, 500 parameters. Municipality 17 → row 17 → [0.2, −1.1, 0.7, 0.0]. A lookup is exactly a one-hot vector times the table (a dense layer with no bias), only faster. It replaces 125 one-hot columns with 4 meaningful numbers.

18Positional encoding

Attention ignores order, so each position gets its own wave pattern

PE(pos) = [ sin(pos), cos(pos) ]
  • Each position gets a unique pattern. Real models use many sine–cosine pairs at different frequencies and add them to the inputs.
  • It is a Fourier basis: fast waves tell neighbours apart, slow waves give the coarse place in the sequence, like the harmonics in spectral analysis.
