Physics vs emulator · why AI weather models are fast

A physics model solves equations at every small time step; an emulator learns one big step

  • Numerical weather prediction (NWP) integrates the equations of motion, thermodynamics and moisture on a grid. Stability limits the time step to minutes, and every step runs the physics (radiation, clouds, convection) on thousands of CPU cores.
  • An AI emulator is trained on decades of ERA5 reanalysis (ECMWF's best estimate of the past atmosphere) to map the state at time t to t + 6 h in one pass of a network on a GPU, then feeds its own output back in (autoregressive rollout).
  • The price: it can only be as good as its training data, it may drift over long runs, and it knows physics only through examples.

5 · Nowcasting · the 0–6 hour problem

The next six hours are hard: storms are small, short-lived and already moving

  • A thunderstorm cell is a few to tens of kilometres across and lives from tens of minutes to a few hours. A 25 km global grid cannot see it; you need a km-scale, convection-allowing model.
  • Nowcasting means forecasting the next 0–6 h. The classic baseline is extrapolation: estimate how the radar echoes move and slide the latest image forward. Very good for the first hour, but it cannot grow, weaken or start a storm.
  • Km-scale NWP (for example NOAA's 3 km HRRR over the US) simulates the storms, but its first hours suffer from spin-up: the model has to build clouds and updrafts that its initial state does not describe well.
  • Earth-2 learns the storm-scale evolution instead: StormCast from an NWP model's output, StormScope straight from satellite and radar.

StormScope = Earth-2 Nowcasting · learning from observations

StormScope learns storms from satellite and radar, not from an NWP state

  • Inputs are GOES geostationary satellite images (several bands) and the MRMS radar mosaic over CONUS, at 6 km. A transformer diffusion model generates the next state; each run is one sample.
  • Two model classes: nowcasting (0–2 h, 10-min steps, the past hour of observations only) and nearcasting (0–12 h, 1-h steps, one GFS channel to steer the large scale).
  • A satellite sees a cloud tower growing before radar sees rain: useful for the hardest part, initiation.

Three nowcasts of the toy world · CSI, probability of exceedance

Moving the echoes beats doing nothing; knowing that storms die and form beats both

Persistence: the issue-time map, unchanged. Advection: motion from cross-correlating two radar frames 30 min apart, then the map slides forward. Generative ensemble (16 members, a toy stand-in for a learned model): each member moves the cells, guesses each cell's remaining life with some noise, and draws new cells at random. CSI = hits / (hits + misses + false alarms) above a threshold.

Claims and their baselines. NVIDIA calls StormScope the "first AI model to outperform physics-based models on short-term precipitation" (vendor claim). The paper (arXiv:2601.17268) says it more carefully: competitive with mesoscale NWP up to 6 h; single-member radar FSS beats HRRR to 3 h and is competitive to 5 h. StormCast's authors report composite-reflectivity FSS comparable to or better than HRRR out to 6 h at 20, 30 and 40 dBZ, with 5-member ensembles that are under-dispersive (too little spread).
